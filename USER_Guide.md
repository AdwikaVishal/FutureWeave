# User Guide — Decision Simulator

> **This is a simulation, not a prediction. The future bends.**

---

## What you're about to do

You're going to describe a real decision you're facing. The simulator will generate three possible 10-year futures for that decision — not to tell you what to do, but to help you see the trade-offs clearly before you make the call.

---

## Step 1 — Describe your decision

Be specific. "Should I take the job at the startup?" works better than "career change".

The more concrete your decision, the more grounded the simulation.

---

## Step 2 — Fill in your context

| Field | What to enter | Why it matters |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| Age | Your current age | Affects income trajectory and time horizon |
| City | Where you live or will live | Used for real salary and cost-of-living data |
| Risk tolerance (1–10) | 1 = very cautious, 10 = all-in | Shapes stress scores and timeline divergence |
| Months of savings | How many months you could survive without income | Affects financial stress in early years |
| Dependents | Number of people financially depending on you | Directly increases stress and living costs |
| Top skills | 1–3 skills most relevant to this decision | Used to ground career growth rates |

There are no right answers. Enter what's true, not what sounds good.

---

## Step 3 — Read the results

### The three timelines

| Timeline | What it optimises |
|----------|-------------------|
| A | Work-life balance — lower stress, slower income growth |
| B | Steady growth — balanced risk, moderate pace |
| C | Aggressive career — faster income, higher stress |

Each timeline shows you Years 1, 3, 5, and 10.

### The scores

Every year shows seven scores (0–100):

- **Income** — relative to local median salary (not raw rupees)
- **Career growth** — promotion speed and skill acquisition
- **Stress** — hours, responsibility, dependents, risk tolerance combined
- **Health** — starts at 70; drops if stress stays above 70 for two or more years
- **Relationships** — time with people you care about; decays with long work hours
- **Happiness** — weighted combination of health (30%), relationships (30%), income (20%), career growth (20%)
- **Opportunity** — probability of a meaningful role change within the next 2 years

A score of 50 is average. 70+ is strong. Below 30 is a warning sign.

### Chaos events

Each timeline includes one unplanned event — a market crash, a mentor, a health scare, a lucky break. These are not random noise. They reflect the realistic probability of disruption over a decade.

### The regret analysis

For each timeline, the simulator identifies:
- **Lost opportunity** — what this path gives up
- **Missed identity** — who you don't become
- **Emotional cost** — how the trade-off shows up day-to-day

These are specific to your context. If you have dependents, the regret will reference them. If your risk tolerance is high, the regret will reflect the safer path not taken.

### The letter from your future self

A 140–200 word letter written from the perspective of you, 10 years from now, in that timeline. The tone is calibrated to the happiness score — proud if things went well, wistful if they were mixed, cautionary if they were hard.

Read it slowly. It's not advice. It's a mirror.

### The hinge point

At the bottom of the comparison, you'll see the single factor that most determines which timeline you end up in. This is the thing worth paying attention to.

---

## Step 4 — What to do with this

- **Don't optimise for the highest score.** A Timeline C with high income and high stress might be exactly right for you — or exactly wrong. Only you know.
- **Look at Year 3, not Year 10.** Year 10 is where things land. Year 3 is where they get hard. That's the year to stress-test.
- **Run it again tomorrow.** Your answers will be slightly different. The simulation will be slightly different. That variance is information.
- **Talk to someone.** If the results surface something uncomfortable, that's the point. Talk to a friend, a mentor, or a counsellor — not to get an answer, but to think out loud.

---

## If you feel overwhelmed

That's a normal response to seeing your future laid out in numbers.

Take a breath. Close the tab. Come back tomorrow.

The simulation will still be there. The decision doesn't have to be made today.

---

## What this is not

- Not a financial plan
- Not a career recommendation
- Not a guarantee of any outcome

The numbers are grounded in real economic data (World Bank, salary databases) and causal rules derived from research. But your life is not a model. Use this as a thinking tool, not a decision engine.

**This is a simulation, not a prediction. The future bends.**
